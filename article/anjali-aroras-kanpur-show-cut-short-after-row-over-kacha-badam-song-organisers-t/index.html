Anjali Arora's Kanpur Show Cut Short After Row Over 'Kacha Badam' Song, Organisers Threaten Legal Action

Influencer Anjali Arora reportedly left a Kanpur dandiya event after about 15 minutes when the DJ played her viral 'Kacha Badam' track, and the event's organiser has accused her of staging a publicity stunt and threatened legal action.

AITeaPost AI• 8h ago • 3 min read

Social media influencer Anjali Arora, who rose to fame through her viral dance video on the song "Kacha Badam," found herself at the centre of a new controversy after a guest appearance at a Nagada Night dandiya event in Kanpur turned tense. Videos from the event, held on Thursday, October 15, at Lajpat Bhawan in Motijheel according to one Hindi report, show Arora growing visibly upset on stage soon after she arrived.

What happened

According to multiple reports, the trouble began when the DJ played "Kacha Badam" — the same song that made Arora a viral sensation years ago — as she stepped onto the stage. Clips circulating online show her stopping her performance, walking toward the DJ console, and speaking agitatedly with the DJ and her own team, some of whom also moved toward the console.

In one widely shared clip, Arora can be heard addressing the crowd, saying, according to a Hindi-language report, that if people in Kanpur behaved this way, celebrities would stop coming to perform, since artists travel for such events because of the love audiences show them. She did not elaborate on-camera, in the footage reviewed by outlets, on exactly what about the song or the moment upset her.

The event's organiser has alleged that Arora left the stage after roughly 15 minutes, even though, he claimed, the booking was for a 40-to-60-minute performance.

The organiser's response

Speaking to reporters after the incident, the event organiser rejected Arora's reaction and called it a "publicity stunt," claiming the team had no idea the song would bother her and had played it precisely because it was the track that first brought her recognition. He said artists should understand that a stage carries its own dignity, and that any grievance ought to be raised with the management afterward rather than on stage.

The organiser also said the event had involved a significant investment, including lakhs of rupees paid to book Arora's appearance, and accused her of deliberately pushing videos of the incident to go viral on social media. He further alleged, without offering independent proof, that a similar episode had occurred during an earlier appearance by Arora in Kanpur, and claimed the controversy had damaged the city's reputation. He said he would pursue legal action against her over the disrupted event.

These claims come from the organiser alone, as reported by outlets including the Free Press Journal and StateMirror, and have not been independently verified. As of this report, Arora has not issued a public statement addressing the organiser's specific allegations, including the claim of a publicity stunt or the threat of legal action.

Reactions and background

The irony of Arora appearing upset over the very song that made her a household name was not lost on social media users, several of whom mocked the moment online, according to StateMirror. The incident also follows recent scrutiny of Arora over her casting as Sita in a mythological stage production, "Mahakavya Shri Ramayan Katha," where some had questioned the choice given her viral dance videos; Arora had responded at the time that she felt no shame about her dancing and that it was simply a hobby.

What's next

Neither side has confirmed whether a formal police complaint or legal notice has actually been filed, despite the organiser's threat. Given that the available reporting leans heavily on the organiser's account and on video clips without full context of what was said at the DJ console, readers should treat the characterisation of Arora's conduct as allegation rather than established fact until she responds or further details emerge.
